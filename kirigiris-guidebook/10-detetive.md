# 10 - Taticas de detetive (secao 09)

A permanent cheat sheet — independent of the case. Six phases: from preparation to the debrief.


## 🔰 For beginners — the detective's partner
_If you're scared to play as Kyoko or Shuichi — become a partner_
Mascote: Makoto Naegi (Ultimate Lucky Student, Danganronpa: Trigger Happy Havoc) - "You don't have to be a detective — it's enough to be nearby and take notes."
(5 pontos)

1. If you're scared to play as Kyoko Kirigiri or Shuichi Saihara right away — pick any character you like
     -- fala de Makoto Naegi (Ultimate Lucky Student)
2. Find a notepad and ask the detective to copy down the fingerprints
3. Write everything down in the notepad: Monokuma events, suspicious behavior, who is where and when
4. Get hold of a UV flashlight:
     subItems: ["Green mesh lockers (×6) → Staff Room", "Metal filing cabinet (×6) → Staff Room", "Lockers (×6) → Technical Room (between 3F and 4F)", "Or: in the Shop for 750 monocoins"]
5. During the investigation — be the detective's partner: collect fingerprints, look for evidence, ask for alibis
     -- fala de Makoto Naegi (Ultimate Lucky Student)

## 🔍 Peacetime / free time
_Gather data while nothing has happened yet_
Mascote: Shuichi Saihara (Ultimate Detective, Danganronpa V3: Killing Harmony) - "Details solve cases. Better to write down too much than to miss what matters."
(32 pontos)

1. Collecting fingerprints from unlocked doors is allowed. But it's better to ask players directly first — if they refuse, lift prints from the places they touched. That way you both interact with the cast and, if you're caught, easily parry with the fact that you had already tried to obtain the prints earlier.
2. Lockpicks can be used to break into a dormitory room door — you don't need to ask permission for this, even at the very start of the game. However, breaking a door open with a crowbar specifically (not picking it with a lockpick) at the very start of the game without an RP reason is considered a violation of rule 4.3.
3. Get hold of: food, zip ties, a bandage, batteries for the UV flashlight. Don't be afraid to ask other players for help
4. Recharge the UV flashlight at the electrical panel or with batteries. It lasts for 20 uses at 5% per collected fingerprint. If you just shine the flashlight, no charge is spent.
5. Copy the notepad's contents to the clipboard ([[CTRL+C]]) — if a culprit edits the text or steals the notepad, you'll be able to restore it ([[CTRL+V]])
     -- fala de Shuichi Saihara (Ultimate Detective)
6. Keep two notepads. The second — in a safe in case of death or theft. Inform trusted players.
     -- fala de Shuichi Saihara (Ultimate Detective)
7. Double-check the recorded fingerprints. You can make a mistake in your notes and then build theories on that mistake, only to discover it at the trial itself.
8. Record the time of Monokuma events. For the masquerade and the blackout, note the start and the end.
9. Close doors — the killer will have to leave a fingerprint to open them, and waste time.
10. Via «Inspect» (the [[T]] button on hover), check players for gloves and night-vision devices, and note them in the notepad
11. Get to know the cast better by talking with players. Watch who sticks with whom and where they go — this helps you understand the dynamics and potentially uncover motives even before a murder
12. Check fingerprints at the workbench in the gym and the medical workbench in the medical office. Keep in mind: a workbench and a medical workbench are also on the 2nd floor of the abandoned dormitory and in the chemistry laboratory (4F).
13. Record players' bloody hands — even if they claim there's no connection to the murder.
14. Players set traps all over the map — especially often inside other players' rooms. A stunned, sedated, or worse, dead detective is no help to anyone. So be careful and watch your step. By the way, the teleport points are fixed — 17 on the first floor + 2 in the boiler room + 1 in the garden by the dormitory, 9 on the second floor, 8 on the third floor, 7 on the fourth floor, and 8 on the fifth floor. Killers can set traps near them in advance, so be careful.
15. Collect gunpowder in the boiler room and the medical office to rule out the making of explosives or a trap. Store it in a safe with yourself or with trusted players.
16. Patrol the school constantly. The mere presence of a detective can prevent a murder or provide important testimony
17. Sometimes you can try to bait players into leaving prints: ask a player to «check» an item or a location under some pretext, ask for some object, or just watch a player and note what they touch. But there's a small risk that someone else's prints were already there, so always double-check or use elimination against what you already know.
18. Zip ties and handcuffs let you restrict a player's actions: they can move, but can't open doors or interact with anything. A bag over the head blocks their view but doesn't stop them from discovering a body. And tape stuck over the mouth prevents speaking in voice chat.
19. You can also tether a player while they're in zip ties or handcuffs. Grab them in a hold and pick the point you want to tie them to. That way they can't run off. And by the way, a teleport event will still move a tethered player — but they'll stay in the zip ties or handcuffs at the new spot.
20. Zip ties are cut with a kitchen knife, a katana, or a cleaver. Handcuffs are opened only with a screwdriver (though you can do it without one if you put them on the player yourself). You can also put them on yourself, but you won't be able to free yourself.
21. Keep in mind that relationships between players are very important, since conflicts and personal animosity can become a motive for murder.
22. Corners are easier to check in third-person thanks to the wider view. But it's better to lift fingerprints in first-person.
23. At first, hit «check the watch» after every encounter with another player; once the habit sets in and you get a better sense of when it's worth checking and when not, you'll use it only as needed.
24. Check the fingerprints on the knife stand in the kitchen: it's the only place in the whole academy where the kitchen knife and the cleaver spawn. This helps narrow the suspect list when the murder weapon is one of those two.
25. Okay, this tip sounds like paranoia, but I still have to write it down: try not to stand still during a conversation — a zip tie goes on in seconds. Move a little from side to side. If you do have to stand, press your back to a wall and control the widest possible field of view. When lifting fingerprints, ask the player to step back a bit. Looks weird? It does. But you're unlikely to get zip-tied.
26. You can use voice-typing software (Wispr Flow, for example) to dictate notes straight into the detective's notepad — it's faster than typing by hand. The program, for the record, only transcribes speech into text.
27. If you're locked in the freezer and not cuffed or zip-tied, you can lay out meat from containers or items from your inventory in the shape of the killer's initials — or, if you have it, just write the killer's name in the notepad.
28. A trap can be disarmed with any object — even a coin, but rectangular props are the handiest: easier to toss and hit the tripwire. With an explosive trap, be careful and keep your distance so the blast wave doesn't catch you. A screwdriver also lets you actually PICK UP a trap.
29. There are four keycards in the game: the staff room, the utility rooms, the abandoned dormitory floor — and the Academy Head's keycard, which combines all three. Every keycard grants access to its namesake location, but the utility-rooms keycard is worth highlighting: it opens the utility room on the 3rd floor and the one between floors 3 and 4. There's also a utility room on the 5th floor, but access there is open even without a keycard. The garden by the dormitory and floors 4 and 5 are reachable only through trials or repairs — keycards don't work there.
30. To lift a fingerprint from surfaces — doors, lockers, the workbench, the medical lab — take the UV flashlight in hand, aim at the print, and press F: the print goes straight into your inventory. How to lift prints from an item: RMB on the item → «Inspect» → hover over the hints until the progress bar fills — properties open up: «Bloodied», «Fingerprints» with a player's letter — or «Cleanup traces» if someone wiped it. To take a print as evidence, press RMB → «Lift fingerprints» → pick the one you need in the submenu. Works with any item.

31. Shoeprints are lifted the same way as fingerprints — when prompted, choose the «shoeprints» profile. There are 16 unique fingerprints and 16 unique shoeprints in the game. Sometimes you can do without a UV scan: say, if a bloody footprint was found next to the body, you can ask several players to step into the blood puddle near the body and compare the prints visually. Then, by elimination, identify the potential killer without collecting a single print.

32. Early-trial strategy: at the start of the game, drop by the tool rack in the shop — the adjustable wrench, hammer, and crowbar spawn there. Write down or memorize what spawned. Then combine that with a hammer check: everyone's own hammer spawns in their cabinet, and if at the trial someone can't produce a hammer and clearly explain where it went — that's already a great lead. In early trials with little evidence, this method can really catch the culprit red-handed. Also keep in mind that bludgeoning weapons spawn in other places too (see below): the hammer also randomly spawns in two toolboxes in the gym (10% each) and on the rack in the shop (35%). Also in the gym — the lead pipe (10%); by the workbench — the baseball bat (20%), which also spawns on the 2nd floor in the giraffe room (20%). The crowbar, besides the rack, can appear in the red locker in the incinerator (15%).

## 🕵 During the investigation
_A body has been found — every minute counts_
Mascote: Chiaki Nanami (Ultimate Gamer, Danganronpa 2: Goodbye Despair) - "Every murder is a level. Find the pattern, and you'll solve it."
(38 pontos)

1. Press G to highlight evidence at the crime scene. Hold the button while you walk around the room — that way you'll spot evidence even behind objects.
2. Ask players not to touch the body, since the killer may have left fingerprints on the victim. Extra touches make finding the killer's prints harder. It rarely works, since everyone usually starts searching the poor soul, but it's still worth a try.
3. There are two kinds of gloves in the game. Plain «gloves» are reusable: while worn, every print you leave is smeared. Disposable gloves also smear prints, but last for 10 uses, after which they tear and from then on leave ordinary prints. Prints, in turn, come in four kinds: ordinary, smeared, bloody, and bloody-smeared. Ordinary and bloody prints let you identify the owner. But ordinary-smeared or bloody-smeared ones, unfortunately, can't be traced to a person. Ordinary and smeared prints (not bloody) can't be seen without a UV flashlight.
4. Breaking in with a crowbar leaves no fingerprints on the door — the killer can use this to hide their presence at that location. But there's a side effect: a loud noise that alerts everyone nearby to the break-in.
5. The «blood puddle» clue confirms that the body wasn't moved and the victim died right there.
6. Traces of traps and throwing weapons help reconstruct the picture: what exactly was used and how much. Downside: they can be wiped away with a mop before you arrive. But luckily, a death description from a trap or a weapon in the Monokuma File can't be wiped away even with a mop.
7. A killer, or some trickster, or sometimes just a troll can wipe evidence with a mop: footprints and the blood puddle, fingerprints and shoeprints, traces of traps and throwing weapons. If you arrive at a murder scene and see none of the above, it doesn't mean it was never there. It may mean someone simply did a thorough job.
8. Check containers' weight and scroll to the bottom cells, or use auto-sort — evidence is often hidden at the bottom. But evidence can also lie around simply as props in stashes — that is, outside a container, behind some object.
9. There are three kinds of shoeprints in the game: bloody (if you stepped in the victim's blood puddle), wet (if you went into the water in the bathhouse or the pool), and dirty (if you were in the garden by the cafeteria or the garden by the dormitory). The effect lasts for 10 steps and then fades, and those 10 steps in turn disappear after 12 in-game hours or 10 real minutes. They're best seen through a UV flashlight.
10. There's always a chance that by the start of the investigation the killer is already dead. For example, the trap could have been set in a previous chapter by the victim or the killer. Another situation: the killer could have committed the murder and then been killed by another player.
11. After striking a blow, the text «bloody hands» appears over the player's head. However, if the blow was struck while wearing gloves, there's no such label — the gloves become bloody, but the hands stay clean. It's also worth noting that bloody gloves can't be washed off in a sink; the most you can do is wipe them with a rag.
12. The bloody-hands effect appears only when you strike a player with a cold weapon BEFORE the investigation (you can't get this effect during the investigation) — not from dragging, not from a punch, and not from picking up a bloodied weapon. Three cases:
     subItems: ["Without gloves and without a disguise — ordinary bloody prints.", "In a disguise without gloves — a smeared bloody print, but the bloody-hands effect remains (take off the disguise and they become ordinary bloody prints).", "With gloves (and in a disguise with gloves) — only the gloves become bloody, the hands are clean. The exception is torn disposable gloves: in them the hands become bloody after a blow, and all smeared prints become bloody. However, once those gloves tear, the prints become ordinary, not bloody, even if you open doors or containers in them."]
13. The «ordinary hit» (LMB), the «swing hit» (when you hold and release LMB), and the right-mouse-button hit are described identically in the Monokuma File. Exception: the katana on a right-mouse-button attack is described differently.
14. If a player dies of hunger or lack of sleep while in zip ties or handcuffs (the Monokuma File should retain marks of the restraints), the guilty one is whoever put them on. And yes, this murder method is a gray area, since by the rules we can't hold another player against their will for more than 5 minutes, but no one says you won't have to deal with cases like this. So forewarned is forearmed.
15. In the case of death from hypothermia in the freezer or overheating in the bathhouse, the guilty one is whoever closed the door.
16. Death from hypothermia occurs after roughly 3 minutes 31 seconds of real time. Overheating kills faster, in roughly 2 minutes 28 seconds of real time. Keeping this in mind, we can look at the victim's time of death and guess when they ended up there.
17. It's useful to keep in mind that the killer gets a notification when a victim triggers their trap. They also have the killer's instinct, which for 20 seconds after a murder shows the positions of other players within a medium radius as a red cloud. This lets them plan an escape route and fabricate an alibi.
18. A killer can restrain themselves — putting on zip ties, handcuffs, tape, a bag — to earn an alibi with the words «I was tied up and couldn't have killed». But the only thing they can take off on their own is the bag.
19. If one of a food's components is poisoned, the food becomes poisoned too. Example: poisoned cheese that is later used to make a sandwich.
20. A single killer, or a killer-and-accomplice pair, can't commit more than two murders per chapter. If there are three or more bodies, then most likely there are at least two independent killers on the server. For this violation there's an automatic mass-kill penalty: when the killer kills a third player, they drop dead themselves and their victims come back to life.
21. Killing a player when there's more than one witness is forbidden. Exception: there are four people in the room — the first is the killer, the second the accomplice, and the killer and accomplice are about to kill the third and fourth. Practical takeaway: you're safe in a group of at least three people, and better — four or more. If the rule is broken, don't hesitate to file a report and attach a recording from the game.
22. A fake alibi doesn't always mean deliberate lying. Players confuse the time, forget where they were, write it down wrong, or misread the in-game clock. First clarify — maybe the person just made a mistake. But it also means you can't always trust players' alibis. At the very least, it's better to have as many people as possible confirm it.
23. Blows to the body after the victim's death leave no wounds — the Monokuma File records only injuries sustained while alive.
24. Damage descriptions in the Monokuma File go in chronological order. But secondary condition details (like being put to sleep) are always appended after the injuries — regardless of when they happened. So if a player was put to sleep with a somnus grenade, woke up, and was then killed — the Monokuma File will show the murder weapon first, and the sleep effect after.
25. The investigation time depends on the opened gates — 7 minutes at the start of the game, +2 minutes for each opened floor, up to a maximum of 15 minutes.
26. During the investigation you can't take damage or die. Stamina, alertness, and satiety aren't spent. You also can't repair the electrical panel, the supercomputer, or the air purifier.
27. While a player is unconscious, they're immune to fall damage, Molotov cocktails, poisons and being crushed. But unfortunately, that still won't save them from explosives or melee weapons.
28. Fists deal 8–12 damage per hit (the same for all characters), but deal no damage to a player at 20 health or below — and yes, you can't kill with fists.
29. Record the time of each repair stage: that way you'll know exactly when the boiler room, the garden by the dormitory, and the upper floors opened. It helps catch false alibis (for example, when someone says «I was by the boiler room», but it hadn't even opened yet at that time) and lets you reconstruct routes: who could even physically have been in the right place at the right time.
30. Establish the alibi time window for this murder. Recall whom you yourself can give an alibi to for that time. How to do it? Take the time of the murder and listen to other players' alibis for that time and around it. Having determined the murder location, hear out the players' testimony and gauge whether any of them could physically have been at the murder scene or not. And most importantly — can anyone confirm their alibi? (one person isn't always enough, since they may turn out to be an accomplice)
31. Check with other players who last saw the victim: when, where, and whether anyone was nearby.
32. Infer the killer's route: where they came from, where they could have washed their hands or the weapon. Sinks are in the restrooms by the gym and the dormitory, on the abandoned floor, on the 2nd and 4th floors, in the medical office, the laundry, the kitchen, the dormitory rooms, the art-room back room, and the chemistry laboratory.
33. If you have time left, check your own room for traps at the end of the investigation. Sometimes a wrongdoer can use the investigation time to set a trap that someone is meant to trigger at the start of the next chapter.
34. The 3rd-floor utility room is a junction of several routes: the gym bridge, the 3rd floor, the vent into the library's archive room, the 2nd-floor men's restroom, the pool, and the boiler room. It lets you move quickly between floors 1, 2, and 3. Keep this in mind when reconstructing the killer's route.
35. A car wheel and a fuel canister work as a step-up and help you reach evidence located above the character model.

37. Monokuma takes away a suicide's body after 5 real minutes along with all its inventory. This makes the body an ideal place to get rid of evidence. The detective, in turn, can dump weapons, traps, and gunpowder there — and check it for evidence too.

## ⚖ During the trial
_Steer the conversation, stick to the facts_
Mascote: Kyoko Kirigiri (Ultimate Detective, Danganronpa: Trigger Happy Havoc) - "Composure. Logic. No gut feelings."
(9 pontos)

1. Note: at the start of the trial, Monokuma tells us exactly whose case we're investigating. This matters, since sometimes there are several bodies and before the trial begins it's unclear — one killer or not.
2. At the start of the trial, immediately announce the murder location, the murder weapon, the time of death, and so on, so everyone is on the same page. Not everyone conducts an investigation, and not everyone understands what's even going on. Also don't forget to state your route (that is, your movement between locations and the exact timings) and your alibi.
3. Please don't wait for someone to say something useful on their own. In the chaos it'll never happen — plus you can simply miss important information when everyone's talking at once. So run the case and bring structure yourself. Ask everyone one at a time. And you don't really have to crack the case single-handedly. Your role is to provide all the information you managed to gather and organize the conversation so that, as a group, you can solve the case. That's what a detective's job is.
4. If the trial descends into chaos and you can't properly talk to a specific player or just voice critical information — call a cross-debate. Everyone goes quiet and the two of you get two minutes one-on-one.
5. Even if you didn't manage to find evidence during the investigation, remember: you're not starting from zero. You have alibis, some of which you probably already gathered during the investigation. Some you can give yourself — you were somewhere too and saw something. The rest of the alibis you close at the trial. And you can start not in order, but with those you suspect most.
6. When there's very little information, watch behavior rather than words. Someone dodges the topic. Someone stays silent when they should answer. Someone tries to seize control of the conversation. These are little clues too.
7. Remember that you can't immediately trust an alibi if only two players confirm it — they could easily turn out to be accomplices.
8. Don't fixate too much on one theory: lock in the current one, and then, if there's time, try to build an alternative, compare — and only then choose.
9. Honestly, a trial without evidence is, unfortunately, the default. And if you've found even one clue, you're already at an advantage. Learn to work through trials purely on testimony and analysis. Once you learn to work with a shortage of information, you'll crack cases with evidence many times faster. If you don't have the information to build a theory — ask for more. Widen the questioning time range: «who was doing what an hour before this», «who saw the victim earlier». If people stay quiet — ask them to say everything they remember, any little detail.

## EVENTOS DO MONOKUMA

- Masquerade [masquerade]: Everyone puts on a disguise and hides their identity.
- Blackout [blackout]: The lights go out across the entire academy.
- Teleport [teleport]: Everyone is randomly teleported to different points on the map.
- Despair Roulette [despair-roulette]: When no murders have happened for a long stretch, the Academy's headmaster puts on a show of his own. One participant becomes the Shooter; everyone else can only hope for luck.

### Dicas

- Events repeat roughly every 12–14 in-game hours. The countdown starts from the beginning of an event, not from its end.
- Even ordinary Monokuma messages (random broadcasts) count as an event.
- Note the moment of every event in your notebook. For a blackout and a masquerade, always log the start and the end: it helps reconstruct alibis and understand when the killer could act unseen.
- Understanding the timings lets you predict the next event and, for example, gather the cast in one place for an alibi.
- Monokuma events do not work during the investigation.
