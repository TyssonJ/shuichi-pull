# Getting started

## How do I start playing?

- Buy and install Garry's Mod on Steam. If you're in Russia, buying it directly won't work — look on dedicated marketplaces such as plati.ru: you buy it there, and a bot automatically gifts the game to your account.

- Launch the game → Multiplayer.

- Search for the Shinri Trial | Danganronpa game mode.

- Choose an empty public lobby from the server list.

- Add-ons will download — on your first join this takes a while.

- Sign in through Discord — create an account or log into an existing one.

- Get familiar with the mechanics on this site or via video: youtu.be/fdiaVeEvqJk <https://youtu.be/fdiaVeEvqJk>

- Look for a game and good luck!

## What age can you play from?

There are no age restrictions, but we recommend ages 14 and up — the game contains themes of violence and murder. Also, taking part in games requires Discord, whose minimum age is 13.

## What would you advise newcomers?

Turn on the monopad right away and keep track of in-game time. Remember where you've been and what you did: at the trial, every detail can play a role.

At the start of the game, stock up on food.

Write all out-of-character lines through /looc. And most importantly — just chat, explore and enjoy yourself. The first games are always chaotic, and that's normal.

## What is Garry's Mod?

Garry's Mod is a sandbox game on Steam (~$10) where people build their own games inside it. Shinri Trial is one of those fan-made modes.

If you'd like to play it: get GMod on Steam → open it → Find Multiplayer Game → pick the Shinri Trial | Danganronpa gamemode → join a lobby. Add-ons download automatically on first join (it takes a while), and you'll need a Discord account to log in. That's it!

## Can I play right now if I don't know Russian?

Yes! There are many English-speaking players and bilingual moderators on the Shinri Trial Global Discord server who can help you get started. Using the video guide and this Guidebook will help you navigate the Russian UI until the English patch is live.

## Do you need to be good at RP, fast typing or voices?

Nope. Heavy RP isn't required in auto games (quick games) — that's the project's official stance. As for fast typing and voices: just be reasonable and everything will be fine. Nobody expects you to have a voice like Megumi Ogata or to type like Chihiro Fujisaki.

Thematically you're playing a student who doesn't know the others either, so being a bit awkward and unsure literally fits the fiction. And first games are chaotic for everyone — that's normal.

## Does the game or the guide contain spoilers for the main series?

The guide focuses on mechanics, but the game itself features characters and environments from the entire series. If you haven't finished the original games, seeing certain characters' bios or locations might be minor spoilers for you.

# Modes & finding a game

## What is quick play / auto-game?

Quick play is an automated mode with no host, completely free and available at any time. The game runs on the classic scheme: crime → investigation → class trial. Exactly 16 players take part, average duration is 1 to 2 hours.

To get into a game, select "Quick play" in the main menu — you'll join a queue. Once 16 people gather, everyone gets a request to confirm participation. If everyone confirms, the server launches automatically and connects the players. If someone doesn't confirm, the queue is reassembled.

After connecting, everyone chooses a character, and the game begins — everyone spawns in their personal rooms.

All events are governed by the system: there is no host, decisions are made only by players, and the pace is faster than in official and private games. Ideal for getting to know the project.

## How do I get into an official game?

The announcement comes out in the official Shinri Trial Discord channel on Sunday at 15:30 Moscow time. Sign-up starts at 16:00 Moscow time — spots fill very quickly, so watch the announcements in advance.

## Where can I find a teammate / a newcomer teammate?

The easiest way is to find a teammate right in the game — in the lobby, or by writing in the #быстрые-игры chats on the official Discord server.

## How can I chat with the cast after the game ends without being whisked straight to the lobby?

After the game ends you have 5 minutes on the server, but you won't be able to run around as your character. Open the console (the ~ key) and type `+voicerecord` — this activates your voice and lets other players hear you. If a prompt to move to the lobby appears, press "No" to stay on the server.

Keep in mind that most players have probably already left the game server. So it's best to arrange in advance in the non-RP chat: "Hey everyone, let's meet in lobby [number] after the game" — otherwise everyone ends up in the lobby they searched for a game from, and the cast may scatter.

## How do I apply to create a private game?

In the Shinri Trial Discord bot: "Menu" → "Start playing" → "Organize a custom game". After you submit, a chat opens where you need to send the filled-out application form.

Form and instructions: blog.shinri-trial.ru/docs/gameplay/community-games <https://blog.shinri-trial.ru/docs/gameplay/community-games/>

## What do I do if I got into an episode/private game but can't make it?

If sign-up isn't closed yet: "Game menu" → "Leave game" — the pass is refunded automatically.

If sign-up is already closed and the reason is valid: "Menu" → "Support" → "Refund" — an administrator will review the request and return the pass.

## What's the main difference between episodes, private games and quick games?

**Quick game (auto-game)** — free, no host, fully automatic. 16 people, classic format, 1–2 hours. Ideal for getting to know the project.

**Episode (official game)** — a story-driven game with live hosts (game masters). A unique scenario, limited spots. There are two ways in: through a lottery (free, spot not guaranteed) or a gold pass — 800 monocoins (≈400 ₽, guaranteed spot).

**Private game (custom game)** — organized by the players themselves, with their own story and rules. To take part you need a silver pass — 600 monocoins (≈300 ₽).

## Are there English-speaking servers, and where do I find them?

Yes — the Shinri Trial GLOBAL Discord server: discord.gg/KYkzy9n3HW <https://discord.gg/KYkzy9n3HW>

## How are quick games (auto-games) run on Shinri Trial Global?

Sign-ups happen in the `future-games` channel — post your UID and your preferred character there. At the moment only pre-planned auto-games are allowed.

## Are there non-RP servers where you can play without roleplaying?

Shinri Trial is an RP project, so playing completely out of character isn't possible. A split into RP and non-RP servers is planned, but no specific timeline has been announced.

# Terms & slang

## What is perception?

A character stat responsible for the evidence-detection radius. The maximum value is 10. For example, Kyoko with perception 10 will spot a piece of evidence from a greater distance than Mondo with perception 3.

## What is a case?

Slang for a murder. "To make a case" = to commit a murder.

## What is a cast?

The cast is the roster of players in your current game. A "great cast" means you got interesting, active or pleasant people. A "lousy cast" is the opposite. Every game is unique precisely because the cast is always different.

# Game mechanics

## How do I use the monopad?

Open the inventory (I), find the monopad, press RMB → "Turn on". After that, pressing C lets you look at your watch and find out the current in-game time.

## How many murders can one player commit?

No more than two per chapter — even with an accomplice.

## Can I use attrition (lack of sleep or hunger) as a method of murder, if the rules forbid holding a player for more than 5 minutes?

Technically attrition exists as a mechanic, but in practice it can't be pulled off — rule 4.11 forbids holding a player longer than 5 minutes, and there are no exceptions for attrition. So this method of murder is unavailable.

## How long until a killer can become a witness?

The killer cannot become the first witness under any circumstances. You can only become a witness after the killer's instinct wears off — 20 real seconds (24 in-game minutes). Once at least one player has discovered the victim, the killer can become the second or third witness, including a simultaneous discovery.

## Where do I find my UID?

In game, at the bottom-left of the screen.

![Where the UID is — bottom-left of the game screen](assets/faq/uid.webp)

## How do I check the time in game?

Two ways: hold C → "Check the time", or open the inventory (I) → RMB on the monopad → "Check the time".

## My emote and animation slots are empty — how do I equip them?

Esc → "Animation editor" → pick the emotes, gestures, poses, dances or walks you want → double-click LMB → choose a slot on the emote wheel to equip it there.

There's a GIF walkthrough in this Guidebook: the Controls <#controls> section → "Mechanics" → "Voice, animations and emotes" → "Animation menu".

## How do I put handcuffs, zip ties, duct tape or a bag on a player?

Walk right up to them, hold E → pick "Restraints" on the interaction wheel → then the item you want (it has to be in your inventory).

The only thing you can take off yourself is the bag. Zip ties are cut with a knife, an axe or a katana; handcuffs come off only with a screwdriver (or without one, if you put them on yourself); duct tape is removed by any other player the same way.

If the player starts moving, the action is cancelled — so try to pull it off unnoticed.

## What actually forms the motive to kill? Are there mechanically enforced motives?

In quick (auto) games there are no mechanically enforced motives — unlike the actual DR games, Monokuma here doesn't hand out motive videos or "if nobody dies, X happens" ultimatums. Nothing in the game forces anyone to kill. It's different in private and official games, where you could and usually do get motives from the GM (game master).

Motives are player-driven: they grow out of the roleplay and social dynamics during the match — a rivalry that builds, a betrayal, someone becoming a threat, self-preservation, or just deciding to play as the blackened and go for the win.

What Monokuma does give you is opportunity, not motive: events like blackout (cover), masquerade (hides identities) and teleport (isolates people and confuses everyone about where the others are) make a kill easier to pull off. The reason comes from the players.

## Is it true there's a taboo that kind characters (like Makoto) shouldn't kill?

It's community etiquette, not an official rule — nobody punishes or tracks it. People break it regularly, which is why you can't work out a suspect from their character choice: absolutely anyone can turn out to be the killer.

What gets frowned upon isn't picking a "kind" character, but killing with no motive and no build-up. If a motive genuinely developed over the course of the game (betrayal, pressure, fear, self-defence and so on), even a classically "good" character killing someone reads as fine.

There is a slight real lean toward canonically suspicious characters (Nagito, Kokichi, Junko and so on) — they're just trusted a little less at their word, but that isn't the same as being instantly suspected of murder.

## Will other maps (like DR2's Jabberwock Island) be added?

Currently the primary map is Hope's Peak Academy from the first game. The developers are considering adding maps from other games in the series as the project grows.

# Disputed situations

## If you stun a player and they die of hunger while unconscious — does that count as murder?

Yes. Since the death resulted from your actions (you stunned them → the victim couldn't eat → they died of hunger), the game counts it as a case and you become the Blackened.

## How long can you keep a player in restraints (handcuffs, zip ties and so on)?

Holding someone for longer than 5 minutes is a rules violation.

## If one player put zip ties on the victim and a different player shut them in the freezer — who is the killer?

The killer is whoever applied the zip ties: that's the first action that restrained the victim.

## Force-feeding food or a drink laced with poison — who's at fault?

If the feeding is forced, the one at fault is whoever did the force-feeding, not whoever poisoned the food.

If the feeding is voluntary (no zip ties, no handcuffs, not asleep), the one at fault is the poisoner, not whoever handed it over.

## Can you hide evidence as a prop?

Yes, you can — but you have to hide it so the item can still be picked up. If a player physically can't reach the hidden evidence, that's a violation. Hiding things inside textures is also not allowed.

## Animations and interactive elements on the site (UV Flashlight, sprite scrolling) don't work — how do I fix it?

Most likely your Windows system animations are disabled. Turn them on:

After that, reload the page.

## If you go as Shuichi/Kyoko and break doors open with a crowbar — is that a violation? And what about lockpicks?

Lockpicks can be used to break into a dormitory room door — you don't need to ask permission for this, even at the very start of the game. However, breaking a door open with a crowbar specifically (not picking it with a lockpick) at the very start of the game without an RP reason is considered a violation of rule 4.3.

## Is hitting someone during the investigation a violation?

Yes, it's a violation of rule 4.2.
